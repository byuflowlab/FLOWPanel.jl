## What was measured

The real R4 lower DAG (1068 leaves, 58,192 unknowns, 48,167 lower block
edges) from `fgs_dag_L_graph_R4.csv` / `fgs_dag_L_edges_R4.csv`, analyzed per
Stage A of
[`fgs_exact_triangular_solver_plan_20260926.md`](021_rotor_hover_solver_benchmarks/fgs_exact_triangular_solver_plan_20260926.md):
contiguous weighted leaf-range partitions $K\in\{2,4,8,16,32,64\}$
(cuts by $w_i = n_i\,\mathrm{ptot}_i + n_i^2$), structural interface set
$C$ = source leaves with outgoing cross-subdomain lower edges, structural
fill of $S = I + R_C B^{-1} E_C$ by reachability propagation through each
target subdomain's local lower DAG, and work-weighted span bounds using the
proxy (gemv $n_t n_s$ per block edge + $n_t^2$ per leaf back-solve).

Headline predecessor measurement (the plan said measure, don't presume ~45):
**mean 45.1, median 48, p90 67, max 121 predecessors/leaf.** Confirmed.

leaf-levels.

## Conclusions presented to Ryan

1. **As the plan specifies (serial block-forward interface solve, the Stage-C
   prototype): guaranteed loss.** Interface work alone
   ($\sim 2.1\times10^8$) exceeds the entire baseline sweep's work; ceiling
   0.29–0.75× at every K, before any overhead/memory/setup. This meets Gate
   A's rejection condition ("the interface is essentially the original
   problem with extra work") with margin.
2. **The parallel-interface rescue isn't a rescue.** Ceiling peaks at ~3.0×
   on the sweep phase (K=32), but only by scheduling ~46k fine-grained S
   blocks carrying a full original-problem's worth of work through a
   dependency DAG — the game dagedge lost and Track A's outside-leaf sync
   just lost (−18.85% at j64 against real 2–3.7× per-leaf ceilings) — plus
   ~2× total work, ~1.6 GB extra storage, and thousands of column-propagation
   setup solves against a total setup B-I1 just drove to 30.5 s. Amdahl
   through the upper products dilutes even a perfect sweep outcome to
   ~1.5–2× solve-level.
3. **Root cause is operator density, not partition choice:** at ~45
   predecessors/leaf, any K-way cut puts 59–91% of unknowns on the
   interface; the span removed from subdomains reappears inside $S$ (the
   bottleneck relocates, Gate C's warned outcome). No partition family
   plausibly escapes a graph this dense.
4. Caveats stated: unweighted-depth skew from the 1,450 tail leaf (addressed
   by the weighted-span table), contiguous partitions only (plan-mandated),
   R4 only (official C-T1 would add R2 + larger meshes; density should worsen,
   not improve, with size).
