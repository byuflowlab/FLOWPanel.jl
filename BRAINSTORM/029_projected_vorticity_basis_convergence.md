## RESET BRIEF

**Question:** FLOWVPM reconstructs vorticity with normalized scalar blobs, but the
Biot–Savart velocity has curl equal to the divergence-free Leray projection of that blob
field. Does the velocity-associated vorticity converge to the intended continuum field,
and does the mismatch warrant a different particle basis?
**Status:** staged on 2026-08-31; no new code, tests, or runs have been performed.
**Parent ruling:** item 010 proved
$\boldsymbol{\omega}_J=\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\times\boldsymbol{u}
=P\boldsymbol{\omega}_\zeta$, found the $2/3$ single-particle self term, and found a
projected-basis overlap solve about 20 times more ill-conditioned on its saved-wake test.
**Prerequisite:** item 009 proved $\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{u}=0$ for the
regularized particle velocity; it did not establish vorticity accuracy.
**Standing fact:** on $\mathbb{R}^3$, the projected tensor basis is nonlocal and not
absolutely integrable, so it does not inherit the scalar blob's ordinary unit-integral
law. Symmetric-volume integration approaches $2\boldsymbol{\Gamma}/3$.
**Positive hypothesis:** if the scalar reconstruction converges in $L^p$, $1<p<\infty$,
to a divergence-free target, boundedness of $P$ implies that its projection also
converges; in $L^2$, projection cannot increase the field error.
**Staged discrepancy ledger:** formalize which direct-basis and projected quantities are
identical at finite resolution, coincide only in a specified refinement topology, or can
remain different because of variable widths, boundaries, tails, or zero-mode conventions.
**Staged circulation audit:** distinguish particle vector strength, direct-basis
vorticity flux, and Kelvin circulation of the computed velocity; determine which is
conserved by evolution, remeshing, projection, and the continuum refinement limit.
**Staged non-conservation audit:** identify the longitudinal/null-space content discarded
by projection, the direct-field moments and norms it changes, and whether latent
longitudinal error can feed future solenoidal dynamics through stretching or SFS updates.
**Next action:** Phase 0 — freeze whole-space versus periodic conventions, norms, coupled
$h$–$\sigma$ limits, the discrepancy ledger, and analytic manufactured fields before
running refinement tests.
**Basis gate:** do not seek a replacement basis unless the assembled projected field has
unacceptable convergence, boundary/zero-mode defects, or a non-vanishing evolution
inconsistency that overlap control and compatible reconstruction cannot remove.

## Objective

Determine whether the VPM scheme converges consistently when its stored/direct vorticity
field is reconstructed from normalized scalar blobs but its velocity is generated by
Biot–Savart and therefore has a different, divergence-free vorticity field. Establish
which field should enter stretching, SFS, diagnostics, and convergence claims, then decide
whether the current basis is adequate or a compatible divergence-free representation is
needed.

## Two vorticity fields in the particle scheme

The direct scalar-basis reconstruction is

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}(\boldsymbol{x})
&= \sum_{p=1}^{N}
   \zeta_{\sigma_p}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{X}_p)\,
   \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p \,.
\end{align}
$$

Because

$$
\begin{align}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^3}
\zeta_\sigma(\boldsymbol{x})\,
\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}
&= 1 \, ,
\end{align}
$$

and consistent quadrature strengths satisfy

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p
&\approx
\boldsymbol{\omega}(\boldsymbol{X}_p)\,\Delta V_p \, ,
\end{align}
$$

the scalar reconstruction has the usual approximate-identity interpretation, provided
particle spacing, core size, weights, and overlap are refined consistently.

Let $G_\sigma$ satisfy

$$
\begin{align}
-\nabla^2G_\sigma
&= \zeta_\sigma \,.
\end{align}
$$

The regularized vector potential and velocity can be written as

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{A}_h(\boldsymbol{x})
&= \sum_{p=1}^{N}
   G_{\sigma_p}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{X}_p)\,
   \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p \, , \\
\boldsymbol{u}_h
&= \boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\times\boldsymbol{A}_h \,.
\end{align}
$$

The vorticity associated with this velocity is

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}
&\equiv \boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\times\boldsymbol{u}_h \\
&= \boldsymbol{\nabla}
   \left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{A}_h\right)
   - \nabla^2\boldsymbol{A}_h \\
&= \sum_{p=1}^{N}
   \left[
      \zeta_{\sigma_p}\boldsymbol{I}
      + \boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\nabla}G_{\sigma_p}
   \right]
   (\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{X}_p)\,
   \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p \\
&= P\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h} \, ,
\end{align}
$$

where $P$ is the Helmholtz–Leray projector onto divergence-free vector fields. In Fourier
space, for $\boldsymbol{k}\ne\boldsymbol{0}$,

$$
\begin{align}
\widehat{\boldsymbol{K}}_\sigma(\boldsymbol{k})
&= \left(
   \boldsymbol{I}
   - \frac{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}^{\mathsf{T}}}
          {|\boldsymbol{k}|^2}
   \right)
   \widehat{\zeta}_\sigma(\boldsymbol{k}) \,.
\end{align}
$$

Thus, the effective basis is the tensor kernel

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{K}_\sigma
&= P\!\left(\zeta_\sigma\boldsymbol{I}\right)
 = \zeta_\sigma\boldsymbol{I}
 + \boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{\nabla}G_\sigma \, ,
\end{align}
$$

not the scalar kernel $\zeta_\sigma\boldsymbol{I}$.

## Why scalar unit integral does not settle projected convergence

The direct basis obeys

$$
\begin{align}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^3}
\zeta_\sigma\boldsymbol{I}\,
\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}
&= \boldsymbol{I} \,.
\end{align}
$$

The projected basis has a long-range Hessian tail proportional to $r^{-3}$ and is not
absolutely integrable on $\mathbb{R}^3$. Consequently, an ordinary unit-integral
statement is not available. Under symmetric finite-volume integration, item 010 found

$$
\begin{align}
\lim_{R\to\infty}
\int_{|\boldsymbol{x}|\le R}
\boldsymbol{K}_\sigma(\boldsymbol{x})\,
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}\,
\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}
&= \frac{2}{3}\boldsymbol{\Gamma} \,.
\end{align}
$$

The Fourier projector is direction-dependent as $\boldsymbol{k}\to\boldsymbol{0}$, so
the zero mode depends on the domain and convention. A periodic solver can define
$P(\boldsymbol{0})=\boldsymbol{I}$ and preserve the mean, but that is not equivalent to
the whole-space symmetric-volume convention.

There is also a no-go result relevant to any proposed replacement. If a matrix kernel is
localized, integrable, sufficiently decaying, and divergence-free column by column, then

$$
\begin{align}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^3}
\boldsymbol{K}(\boldsymbol{x})\,
\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}
&= \boldsymbol{0} \,.
\end{align}
$$

Therefore, no basis can simultaneously be local and $L^1$, exactly divergence-free, and
carry the same nonzero zeroth moment as a normalized scalar blob. A different basis would
have to relinquish at least one of those properties or reinterpret what
$\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p$ represents.

## Why convergence may nevertheless hold

Let the continuum target $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ be divergence-free. If the scalar particle
reconstruction converges in a topology where $P$ is bounded, then

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
&\longrightarrow \boldsymbol{\omega}
\end{align}
$$

implies

$$
\begin{align}
P\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
&\longrightarrow P\boldsymbol{\omega}
 = \boldsymbol{\omega} \,.
\end{align}
$$

For $1<p<\infty$, the Leray projector is bounded in $L^p$. In $L^2$, it is an
orthogonal projection, giving the especially strong estimate

$$
\begin{align}
\left\|
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}-\boldsymbol{\omega}
\right\|_{L^2}
&= \left\|
P\left(
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}-\boldsymbol{\omega}
\right)
\right\|_{L^2} \\
&\le
\left\|
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}-\boldsymbol{\omega}
\right\|_{L^2} \,.
\end{align}
$$

Hence, the failure of one projected particle to have unit integral does not by itself
prevent convergence of the assembled field. The longitudinal components of neighboring
particle errors can cancel or be removed by $P$, while the projected sum approaches the
divergence-free target.

This is only a conditional result. Weak convergence of the raw delta measure alone does
not imply pointwise convergence after differentiation or application of singular integral
operators. The usual particle conditions still matter:

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p
&\sim \boldsymbol{\omega}(\boldsymbol{X}_p)\,h^3 \, , \\
\frac{h}{\sigma}
&\longrightarrow 0 \, , \\
\sigma
&\longrightarrow 0 \,.
\end{align}
$$

At fixed $\sigma$, refinement can converge only to a filtered field. At fixed
$\sigma/h$, a non-vanishing particle quadrature error may remain unless the kernel and
particle layout satisfy a separate consistency condition. Projection removes only the
longitudinal part of the error; it does not remove smoothing bias, particle quadrature
error, boundary error, or error already lying in the solenoidal subspace.

## Staged discrepancy ledger: what becomes the same and what does not?

The phrase “the fields converge” is insufficient unless it identifies the field, limit,
norm, domain, and observable. This section stages a formal ledger of the relationships
between the direct scalar-basis field and the velocity-associated projected field before,
during, and after refinement.

Define the longitudinal discrepancy

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{g}_h
&\equiv
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h} \\
&= \left(\boldsymbol{I}-P\right)
   \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h} \\
&= \boldsymbol{\nabla}\phi_h \, ,
\end{align}
$$

with the potential, boundary conditions, and zero mode determined by the chosen domain.
Then

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
&= \boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}+\boldsymbol{g}_h \, , \\
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}
&= 0 \,.
\end{align}
$$

The ledger must distinguish four refinement regimes.

### Regime A — finite particle resolution

At finite $h$ and $\sigma$, the two vorticity fields are generally different. However,
some quantities agree exactly because Biot–Savart annihilates the longitudinal component:

$$
\begin{align}
\operatorname{BS}
\left[\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}\right]
&= \operatorname{BS}
\left[P\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}\right] \\
&= \operatorname{BS}
\left[\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}\right] \\
&= \boldsymbol{u}_h \,.
\end{align}
$$

This identity assumes that Biot–Savart and $P$ use compatible domains, boundary
conditions, and harmonic/zero-mode conventions. It means the direct and projected fields
generate the same calculated velocity at every resolution; it does not mean that they
give the same local vorticity, divergence, stretching, or SFS term.

On a periodic domain or in free space with sufficient decay, the projection is
$L^2$-orthogonal. For every divergence-free test field $\boldsymbol{v}$,

$$
\begin{align}
\left\langle
\boldsymbol{v},
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
\right\rangle
&= \left\langle
\boldsymbol{v},
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}
\right\rangle \,.
\end{align}
$$

The enstrophy-like $L^2$ norms instead satisfy the orthogonal decomposition

$$
\begin{align}
\left\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}\right\|_{L^2}^2
&= \left\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}\right\|_{L^2}^2
 + \left\|\boldsymbol{g}_h\right\|_{L^2}^2 \,.
\end{align}
$$

Thus, projection removes longitudinal enstrophy. It leaves the velocity unchanged but can
change any diagnostic or closure that uses vorticity magnitude directly.

### Regime B — particle refinement at fixed uniform filter width

If particle quadrature converges while a homogeneous $\sigma$ remains fixed, then

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
&\longrightarrow
\zeta_\sigma*\boldsymbol{\omega} \,.
\end{align}
$$

For a divergence-free target and a homogeneous convolution filter,

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot
\left(\zeta_\sigma*\boldsymbol{\omega}\right)
&= \zeta_\sigma*
   \left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}\right) \\
&= 0 \,.
\end{align}
$$

Consequently,

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}
&=P\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
\longrightarrow
\zeta_\sigma*\boldsymbol{\omega} \,.
\end{align}
$$

Both fields converge to the same filtered continuum field if the scalar quadrature error
vanishes in a topology where $P$ is bounded. They do not converge to the unfiltered field
unless $\sigma$ is subsequently reduced.

### Regime C — joint particle and filter refinement

If


$$
\begin{align}
h
&\longrightarrow0 \, , \\
\sigma
&\longrightarrow0 \, , \\
\frac{h}{\sigma}
&\longrightarrow0 \, ,
\end{align}
$$

and the kernel, quadrature, domain, and target have the required regularity, then the
intended conclusion is

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
&\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\omega} \, , \\
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}
&\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\omega} \, , \\
\boldsymbol{u}_h
&\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{u} \,.
\end{align}
$$

The norm matters. $L^2$ convergence of vorticity does not by itself imply pointwise
convergence at particle centers or convergence of vorticity gradients. Distributional
convergence implies that the direct-field divergence tends to zero only distributionally;
strong convergence of divergence requires additional derivative control, such as an
appropriate $H^1$ or $H(\operatorname{div})$ estimate.

### Regime D — limits in which the discrepancy can persist

The two fields need not approach the same limit in any of the following cases:

- $\sigma/h$ is held at a value with non-vanishing particle quadrature error;
- particle disorder or incorrect volume weights prevent consistency;
- the effective filter width varies spatially, so filtering does not preserve
  divergence;
- boundaries or truncation alter the domain-specific projection;
- the whole-space and periodic zero modes are compared using different conventions; or
- the nonlinear evolution uses the two vorticity fields inconsistently and the resulting
  trajectory error does not vanish with refinement.

For a genuinely variable observation-point filter, it is possible that

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
&\longrightarrow
\overline{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{\sigma(\boldsymbol{x})} \, , \\
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}
&\longrightarrow
P\overline{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{\sigma(\boldsymbol{x})} \, ,
\end{align}
$$

with

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot
\overline{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{\sigma(\boldsymbol{x})}
&\ne0 \,.
\end{align}
$$

In that case the two limits are physically different even after particle quadrature has
fully converged. Item 028 owns the corresponding variable-filter commutation question.

## Staged circulation audit: direct basis versus computed velocity

The word “circulation” can refer to several different quantities in a particle method.
This section stages a formal separation of those quantities and an audit of which
conservation statements the current scheme actually satisfies.

### Particle vector strength is not automatically Kelvin circulation

For the three-dimensional scalar particle basis,

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}(\boldsymbol{x})
&= \sum_p
   \zeta_{\sigma_p}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{X}_p)\,
   \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p \, ,
\end{align}
$$

normalization gives

$$
\begin{align}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^3}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}(\boldsymbol{x})\,
\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}
&= \sum_p\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p \,.
\end{align}
$$

Thus $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p$ is a vector weight approximating volume-integrated
vorticity. In three dimensions it has units

$$
\begin{align}
\left[\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p\right]
&= \frac{L^3}{T} \, ,
\end{align}
$$

whereas Kelvin circulation has units

$$
\begin{align}
\left[\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{C}}\right]
&= \frac{L^2}{T} \,.
\end{align}
$$

The normalized volume integral therefore does not make an individual three-dimensional
particle strength equal to circulation around a loop. This differs from two-dimensional
vortex particles, whose scalar strengths can directly carry circulation because the
kernel is normalized over area.

The symbol $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p$ must not be interpreted as a Kelvin circulation merely
because it is traditionally called a particle “circulation” or “strength.” Its connection
to circulation is mediated by reconstructed vorticity flux and the geometry of a chosen
surface.

### Circulation of the computed velocity belongs to the projected vorticity

For a closed loop $\mathcal{C}$ and an oriented spanning surface $S$ with
$\partial S=\mathcal{C}$, define the circulation of the computed velocity by

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{G}_u(\mathcal{C})
&\equiv \oint_{\mathcal{C}}
   \boldsymbol{u}_h\!\cdot\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\ell} \,.
\end{align}
$$

Stokes' theorem gives

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{G}_u(\mathcal{C})
&= \int_S
   \left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\times\boldsymbol{u}_h\right)
   \!\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\,
   \mathrm{d}S \\
&= \int_S
   \boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\,
   \mathrm{d}S \,.
\end{align}
$$

Therefore, the vorticity flux exactly associated with the calculated velocity is the flux
of $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}$, not the flux of
$\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}$.

The circulation-like flux implied by the direct basis is

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{G}_\zeta(S)
&\equiv \int_S
   \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\,
   \mathrm{d}S \,.
\end{align}
$$

Using
$\boldsymbol{g}_h=\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}$,
their finite-resolution discrepancy is

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{G}_\zeta(S)-\mathcal{G}_u(\mathcal{C})
&= \int_S
   \boldsymbol{g}_h\!\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\,
   \mathrm{d}S \\
&= \int_S
   \boldsymbol{\nabla}\phi_h\!\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\,
   \mathrm{d}S \,.
\end{align}
$$

Projection does not remove circulation from the computed velocity. The longitudinal field
$\boldsymbol{g}_h$ already lies in the null space of the compatible Biot–Savart operator:

$$
\begin{align}
\operatorname{BS}[\boldsymbol{g}_h]
&= \boldsymbol{0} \,.
\end{align}
$$

It therefore contributes neither velocity nor velocity-loop circulation. Projection makes
explicit which part of the direct reconstruction can be the curl of the calculated
velocity.

### Surface dependence exposes the direct field's divergence defect

For a divergence-free vorticity field, flux through any two surfaces spanning the same
loop is identical. Let $S_1$ and $S_2$ share boundary $\mathcal{C}$ and together enclose a
volume $V_{12}$ with consistent orientation. Then

$$
\begin{align}
\int_{S_1}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_1\,
\mathrm{d}S
-\int_{S_2}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_2\,
\mathrm{d}S
&= \int_{V_{12}}
   \boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}\,
   \mathrm{d}V \\
&=0 \,.
\end{align}
$$

The corresponding direct-basis fluxes satisfy

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{G}_\zeta(S_1)-\mathcal{G}_\zeta(S_2)
&= \int_{V_{12}}
   \boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot
   \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}\,
   \mathrm{d}V \,.
\end{align}
$$

Hence the direct basis generally does not imply a unique circulation for a loop at finite
resolution: its inferred value can depend on the selected spanning surface. The projected
field does imply a surface-independent circulation, subject to the domain's topology and
boundary conditions.

This gives a physically interpretable convergence diagnostic. If the direct reconstruction
converges strongly enough to a divergence-free target, then for fixed admissible surfaces

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{G}_\zeta(S_1)
-\mathcal{G}_\zeta(S_2)
&\longrightarrow0 \, , \\
\mathcal{G}_\zeta(S)
-\mathcal{G}_u(\mathcal{C})
&\longrightarrow0 \,.
\end{align}
$$

Weak or $L^2$ volume convergence alone may not control arbitrary surface traces; the
formal proof must identify sufficient $H(\operatorname{div})$, Sobolev, or smooth-kernel
regularity.

### Global vorticity moment is not loop circulation

The direct identity

$$
\begin{align}
\int_{\mathbb{R}^3}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}\,
\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}
&= \sum_p\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p
\end{align}
$$

is a zeroth-moment conservation statement. It is not Kelvin's theorem. The projected
whole-space basis has a non-absolutely-integrable tail and, under symmetric-volume
integration, instead gives

$$
\begin{align}
\lim_{R\to\infty}
\int_{|\boldsymbol{x}|\le R}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}\,
\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}
&= \frac{2}{3}
   \sum_p\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p \,.
\end{align}
$$

This $2/3$ relation does not mean that the velocity has lost one third of its circulation.
The whole-volume vector moment is not the flux through a specified vortex-tube
cross-section and is not the line integral around a specified loop. Moreover, the
projected moment depends on the whole-space integration convention, while periodic
projection can preserve the zero mode by setting
$P(\boldsymbol{0})=\boldsymbol{I}$.

For sufficiently localized, divergence-free continuum vorticity, the total vector
integral is itself constrained to vanish under appropriate decay. In such a case both
direct and projected global moments may approach zero even though nonzero local loop
circulations and vortex-tube fluxes remain.

### Conservation during particle evolution

Kelvin's theorem concerns a material loop $\mathcal{C}(t)$ transported by the velocity.
For inviscid Euler flow with conservative body forces,

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}
\oint_{\mathcal{C}(t)}
\boldsymbol{u}\!\cdot\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\ell}
&=0 \,.
\end{align}
$$

This does not require each three-dimensional particle vector strength to remain constant.
Under vortex stretching, a particle strength can change according to a relation of the
form

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p}{\mathrm{d}t}
&\approx
\left(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\nabla}\right)
\boldsymbol{u}_h \,.
\end{align}
$$

Preserving $\sum_p\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p$ through remeshing, splitting, or merging is useful
but is neither necessary nor sufficient by itself to prove discrete Kelvin circulation
conservation. Conversely, changes in individual $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_p$ do not by
themselves imply loss of Kelvin circulation.

With viscosity, SFS forcing, non-conservative forcing, or numerical regularization, the
material-loop circulation can change. Schematically,

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{G}_u}{\mathrm{d}t}
&= \oint_{\mathcal{C}(t)}
   \left(
      \nu\nabla^2\boldsymbol{u}
      + \boldsymbol{f}_{\mathrm{SFS}}
      + \boldsymbol{f}_{\mathrm{other}}
   \right)
   \!\cdot\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\ell} \,.
\end{align}
$$

The audit must compare observed circulation change with the change predicted by the
specific continuum model rather than demanding zero circulation change in a viscous or
modeled calculation.

### Topology and boundary qualifications

On a simply connected free-space domain with decay, curl and boundary conditions largely
fix the velocity. On periodic or multiply connected domains, harmonic velocity components
can carry loop circulation without being determined by vorticity. Accordingly:

- equality of vorticity fields does not determine circulation around non-contractible
  loops unless harmonic modes are also specified;
- $P(\boldsymbol{0})$ and the mean flow must be recorded in periodic tests; and
- solid boundaries require the projection, Biot–Savart solve, and Stokes surfaces to obey
  compatible boundary conditions.

### Staged circulation tests

The item will add the following tests to Phases 1–3:

1. **Static Stokes closure:** for prescribed loops and multiple spanning surfaces, verify
   that line circulation of $\boldsymbol{u}_h$ equals flux of
   $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}$ to numerical tolerance.
2. **Direct-flux discrepancy:** measure

$$
\begin{align}

&= \frac{
   \left|
   \mathcal{G}_\zeta(S)-\mathcal{G}_u(\mathcal{C})
   \right|}

$$

   and verify its equality to the longitudinal flux.
3. **Surface-independence defect:** measure

\end{align}
$$

   and compare it with the volume integral of
   $\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}$ between the surfaces.

   modes so that vorticity-based and velocity-based circulation claims cannot be confused.

The circulation audit must conclude which quantity the implementation conserves, which
quantity the governing equation requires it to conserve, and whether their discrepancy
vanishes along the production refinement path.

## Staged non-conservation and null-space audit

Projection preserves the velocity-producing part of the direct vorticity field, but it
does not preserve every property of that field. This section stages an audit of what is
discarded, whether that discarded content should ever have been regarded as physical, and
whether it can influence later dynamics despite being invisible to the instantaneous
Biot–Savart velocity.

### The longitudinal component is the discarded quantity

Let

$$
\begin{align}
P
&= \text{the domain-specific Leray projector} \, , \\
Q
&\equiv \boldsymbol{I}-P \,.
\end{align}
$$

The direct vorticity decomposes as

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
&= P\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}
 + Q\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h} \\
&= \boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}
 + \boldsymbol{g}_h \, , \\
\boldsymbol{g}_h
&= \boldsymbol{\nabla}\phi_h \,.
\end{align}
$$

In Fourier space, for $\boldsymbol{k}\ne\boldsymbol{0}$,

$$
\begin{align}
\widehat{\boldsymbol{g}}_h(\boldsymbol{k})
&= \frac{\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{k}^{\mathsf{T}}}
        {|\boldsymbol{k}|^2}
   \widehat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{\zeta,h}(\boldsymbol{k}) \,.
\end{align}
$$

This is the longitudinal component: the part parallel to
$\boldsymbol{k}$ and determined by the divergence defect. It cannot be the curl of a
velocity field because

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot
\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\times\boldsymbol{u}\right)
&=0 \,.
\end{align}
$$

Accordingly,

$$
\begin{align}
\operatorname{BS}[\boldsymbol{g}_h]
&=\boldsymbol{0} \,.
\end{align}
$$

The compatible Biot–Savart velocity depends only on
$\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}=P\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}$. The longitudinal component is
therefore a velocity-null-space component, not a missing contribution to the instantaneous
velocity.

### “Discarded by projection” is not the same as deleted from the particle state

In the current conceptual scheme, projection occurs implicitly when the direct particle
field is passed through Biot–Savart: the velocity ignores $\boldsymbol{g}_h$. Unless the
particle strengths are explicitly projected or reconstructed, however,
$\boldsymbol{g}_h$ remains encoded in the stored direct-basis field.

The method can therefore have two simultaneous states:

- an **active transverse state** $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}$ that produces velocity; and
- a **latent longitudinal state** $\boldsymbol{g}_h$ that produces no instantaneous
  velocity but may still enter any update written directly in terms of
  $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}$ or the particle strengths.

Calling the Biot–Savart projection “dissipation” would be misleading. The velocity and its
kinetic energy are unchanged because $\boldsymbol{g}_h$ never contributed to them. What is
reduced is direct-field vorticity content, such as its $L^2$ norm, local magnitude, and
surface flux.

### Quantities not preserved by projection

Under periodic or sufficiently decaying free-space assumptions, $P$ is an orthogonal
projection in $L^2$. Consequently,

$$
\begin{align}
\left\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}\right\|_{L^2}^2
&= \left\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}\right\|_{L^2}^2
 + \left\|\boldsymbol{g}_h\right\|_{L^2}^2 \,.
\end{align}
$$

Projection does not generally preserve:

- pointwise vorticity magnitude or direction;
- vorticity at particle centers, including the single-particle self term;
- direct-field enstrophy;
- flux of the direct basis through an arbitrary open surface;
- surface independence of that direct-basis flux;
- the scalar basis's whole-volume vector moment or unit-integral interpretation;
- local helicity density;
- vortex-stretching and SFS terms evaluated with the direct vorticity; or
- individual particle coefficients in any explicit projected reconstruction.

By contrast, a compatible projection does preserve:

- the induced Biot–Savart velocity;
- kinetic energy computed from that unchanged velocity;
- the transverse Fourier component of vorticity;
- line circulation computed from the velocity;
- projected-vorticity flux through a spanning surface; and
- under suitable boundary assumptions, global inner products with divergence-free test
  fields, including global helicity with the same solenoidal velocity.

The audit must verify each statement for the actual domain and implementation rather than
assuming free-space and periodic identities are interchangeable.

### Is longitudinal vorticity conserved by the continuum equation?

Physical vorticity satisfies the solenoidal constraint. For the incompressible inviscid
vorticity equation

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{D\boldsymbol{\omega}}{Dt}
&= \left(\boldsymbol{\omega}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\nabla}\right)
   \boldsymbol{u} \, , \\
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{u}
&=0 \, ,
\end{align}
$$

taking the divergence gives

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{D}{Dt}
\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}\right)
&=0 \,.
\end{align}
$$

Thus, exact Euler evolution preserves the constraint
$\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}=0$. If a nonzero divergence defect is
inserted into the same continuum equation, that scalar defect is materially transported
rather than automatically projected away.

With viscosity,

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{D}{Dt}
\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}\right)
&= \nu\nabla^2
   \left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}\right)
\end{align}
$$

under the standard constant-viscosity equation, so the divergence defect diffuses. SFS,
variable filtering, particle operations, boundaries, and inconsistent discrete updates can
add further sources.

This does not imply that the nonlocal field
$\boldsymbol{g}_h=Q\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}$ has a separately conserved norm or
shape. Helmholtz decomposition depends on the full field and domain. The conserved or
transported continuum constraint is the divergence scalar; the longitudinal energy can
redistribute nonlocally as the field evolves.

### A velocity-null component can contaminate future velocity

Although $\boldsymbol{g}_h$ produces no velocity at one instant, it need not be dynamically
harmless if the particle update uses the direct field. Let the physical or modeled
vorticity update be represented schematically by

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\omega}}{\partial t}
&= \mathcal{F}
   \left(\boldsymbol{\omega},\boldsymbol{u}\right) \,.
\end{align}
$$

Updating the direct field and then projecting produces the transverse tendency

$$
\begin{align}
P\mathcal{F}
\left(
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}+\boldsymbol{g}_h,
\boldsymbol{u}_h
\right) \,.
\end{align}
$$

A compatible projected update would instead use

$$
\begin{align}
P\mathcal{F}
\left(
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h},
\boldsymbol{u}_h
\right) \,.
\end{align}
$$

Their difference is

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{R}_{L\rightarrow T}
&\equiv P\left[
\mathcal{F}
\left(
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}+\boldsymbol{g}_h,
\boldsymbol{u}_h
\right)
-\mathcal{F}
\left(
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h},
\boldsymbol{u}_h
\right)
\right] \,.
\end{align}
$$

For the stretching term, the leading discrepancy is

$$
\begin{align}
\boldsymbol{R}_{L\rightarrow T}^{\mathrm{stretch}}
&= P\left[
\left(\boldsymbol{g}_h\!\cdot\boldsymbol{\nabla}\right)
\boldsymbol{u}_h
\right] \,.
\end{align}
$$

This projected term can be nonzero. The longitudinal component can therefore be converted
by the nonlinear update into a transverse component that affects future velocity, even
though it did not affect the velocity at the beginning of the step. An analogous channel
may exist in an SFS closure if the closure reads direct particle vorticity, its magnitude,
or its direction.

This conversion mechanism is the primary dynamical implication to test. If
$\boldsymbol{g}_h$ merely remains a decoupled null-space component and vanishes under
refinement, it may be benign. If the update repeatedly converts it into transverse
vorticity, it becomes a consistency error in the velocity evolution.

### Null-space drift and observability

Because different direct vorticity fields separated by a gradient generate the same
velocity, velocity-only diagnostics cannot detect longitudinal drift. A simulation can
show apparently converged velocity or force histories while its stored particle field
accumulates large direct-field divergence, enstrophy, or cancellation.

Possible consequences include:

- SFS activation driven by nonphysical direct-field magnitude;
- biased stretching when direct vorticity is used as the stretching direction;
- growing sensitivity of particle coefficients to remeshing or overlap solves;
- disagreement between direct-basis flux and velocity-loop circulation;
- misleading vorticity visualizations despite an accurate velocity field;
- loss of convergence when latent longitudinal content is converted into transverse
  content; and
- poor conditioning caused by trying to recover coefficients for velocity-null modes.

Conversely, explicitly deleting longitudinal content can alter direct-field diagnostics
and particle moments without changing the instantaneous velocity. Any projection or
cleaning operation must therefore report both what it fixes and what representation-level
information it discards.

### Staged non-conservation diagnostics

The item will add the following measurements to Phases 1–3:

$$
\begin{align}
r_L
&= \frac{
   \left\|\boldsymbol{g}_h\right\|_{L^2}}
   {\left\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}\right\|_{L^2}+\omega_0} \, , \\
E_L
&= \frac{1}{2}
   \left\|\boldsymbol{g}_h\right\|_{L^2}^2 \, , \\
e_{\mathrm{div},-1}
&= \frac{
   \left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\cdot
   \boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}\right\|_{H^{-1}}}
   {\left\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}\right\|_{L^2}+\omega_0} \, , \\
r_{L\rightarrow T}
&= \frac{
   \left\|\boldsymbol{R}_{L\rightarrow T}\right\|_{L^2}}
   {\left\|P\mathcal{F}
   \left(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h},\boldsymbol{u}_h\right)
   \right\|_{L^2}+f_0} \,.
\end{align}
$$

On compatible periodic/free-space domains,
$\|\boldsymbol{g}_h\|_{L^2}$ and the appropriate homogeneous
$H^{-1}$ norm of the divergence are equivalent representations of the same longitudinal
error; the exact zero-mode convention must be stated.

For every time step and particle operation, record a budget containing:

- change in longitudinal energy $E_L$;
- change in transverse vorticity and velocity;
- predicted longitudinal-to-transverse conversion
  $\boldsymbol{R}_{L\rightarrow T}$;
- direct and projected enstrophy;
- direct-field divergence norm;
- particle vector-strength moment;
- direct and projected circulation diagnostics from the preceding section; and
- changes caused specifically by advection, stretching, diffusion, SFS, remeshing,
  splitting, merging, and truncation.

### Mitigation ladder if the discarded content is harmful

Mitigations must be tested in increasing order of invasiveness:

1. **Observe only:** retain the current scheme but monitor $r_L$ and
   $r_{L\rightarrow T}$ if both vanish at the expected refinement rate.
2. **Use compatible fields in nonlinear terms:** compute stretching and SFS inputs from
   $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}$ while retaining scalar particles for storage and
   quadrature.
3. **Divergence cleaning or constrained remeshing:** remove longitudinal content after
   particle operations while preserving the velocity-producing component and selected
   physical invariants.
4. **Projected coefficient recovery:** explicitly represent
   $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}$, but only with a conditioning strategy that resolves item
   010's negative overlap-solve result.
5. **Compatible basis replacement:** pursue the alternative representations in Phase 4
   only if longitudinal error or its nonlinear conversion remains leading order at
   feasible refinement.

An explicit projection must not be accepted merely because it reduces direct enstrophy.
It must improve a velocity-, circulation-, or evolution-consistency metric without
introducing unacceptable boundary, moment, conditioning, or cost errors.

### Decision rules for the longitudinal component

- **Benign representation error:** $r_L\to0$ at the expected rate,
  $r_{L\rightarrow T}$ is asymptotically subleading, and velocity/circulation convergence
  is unaffected. Monitor and document; do not change the basis.
- **Latent but dynamically active error:** velocity is instantaneously unchanged, but
  $r_{L\rightarrow T}$ is comparable to the physical stretching or SFS tendency. Use
  compatible fields in the update or add cleaning before considering a new basis.
- **Operation-generated error:** remeshing, splitting, merging, or variable-width changes
  create jumps in $E_L$. Correct that operation and retest convergence.
- **Persistent formulation error:** $r_L$ or its transverse conversion plateaus under the
  production refinement path. Escalate to projected recovery or a compatible basis.
- **Diagnostic-only discrepancy:** direct vorticity plots or moments differ while all
  velocity-primary and circulation metrics converge. Reinterpret the direct field rather
  than claiming physical non-conservation.

   convention; and

## Phase 4 — basis and formulation decision

Consider alternatives only after Phases 1–3 identify a persistent error. Candidate routes
include:

### A. Retain the scalar particle basis and make the velocity-primary interpretation explicit

Treat $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\zeta,h}$ as a quadrature/reconstruction field and use
$\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}=\boldsymbol{\nabla}\!\times\boldsymbol{u}_h$ whenever an
exactly compatible vorticity is required. This is the minimum-change route if both fields
converge and their evolution mismatch vanishes.

### B. Retain scalar particles but constrain the assembled field

Use remeshing, compatible strength recovery, or a discrete divergence constraint to reduce
the longitudinal component before Biot–Savart projection. This may recover most of the
benefit without solving directly in the poorly conditioned projected basis.

### C. Use the existing projected tensor basis explicitly

Represent vorticity with $\boldsymbol{K}_\sigma=P(\zeta_\sigma\boldsymbol{I})$. This is
exactly compatible with the velocity but nonlocal, has a domain-dependent zero mode, lacks
the scalar unit-moment interpretation, and produced a badly conditioned overlap solve in
item 010. Any renewed attempt requires preconditioning or a different coefficient
formulation.

### D. Adopt a different compatible representation

Explore vector-potential particles, divergence-free matrix radial basis functions,
compatible finite-element/de Rham constructions, vortex segments, rings, or wavelets.
These bases change locality, coefficient meaning, or zeroth moments; none may be described
as a drop-in normalized scalar blob. Compare conservation, conditioning, computational
cost, FMM compatibility, remeshing, and SFS closure implications.

## Decision rules

- **Retain the current basis** if both vorticity fields and velocity converge at acceptable
  rates, the direct/projected difference vanishes under the production refinement path,
  and mixed-field evolution introduces no leading-order error.
- **Retain but reinterpret** if velocity and $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{J,h}$ converge while the
  direct field is only an auxiliary quadrature quantity. Update diagnostics and model
  derivations to use the compatible field where required.
- **Improve particle consistency first** if the dominant error is longitudinal and falls
  under remeshing, overlap control, moment correction, or a discrete divergence constraint.
- **Investigate a new basis** only if the projected field or velocity has unacceptable
  convergence, boundary or zero-mode errors persist, or the mixed-field evolution error
  does not vanish at feasible resolution.
- **Reject a candidate basis** if it claims simultaneous locality, absolute integrability,
  exact divergence freedom, and a nonzero scalar-style unit integral without explicitly
  resolving the no-go result above.
