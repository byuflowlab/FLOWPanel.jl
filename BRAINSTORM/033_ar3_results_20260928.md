## Task 1 — instrumentation check (load-bearing for the gate)

Read `benchmark/fgs_coop_executor_ar2.jl` end to end for `MODE=time`. Finding:
**trial-phase solve timings are uninstrumented.** The in-situ `h_w` probe
(`insitu_hw!`, lines 193–231) runs as a *separate, earlier pass*: it starts its
own team (`FM.dagteam_start_team!`), times isolated `dagteam_pull!` /
`dagteam_coop_dispatch!`/`wait!`/reduction calls directly, then tears the team
down (`dagteam_stop_team!`) — entirely before the timing loop begins. The
paired warmup+trial loop (lines 246–276) calls `cold_solve!` →
`pnl._solve!(rotor, solver)`, wrapped only in `@elapsed` at whole-solve
granularity; no per-leaf/per-pull hooks are active during these solves. So the
trial `solve_seconds` rows are genuine uninstrumented wall-clock, and the A-G
gate (defined on "paired uninstrumented wall clock") applies to them directly
and unconditionally — no conditional flag needed.

## In-situ h_w vs local estimates

Per-leaf cooperative-pull overhead (`h_w = t_coop − t_seq/w`), measured on
the live R4 DAG (1,112 leaves with ptot>0; probed at median/p90/max `nof`
leaf classes), 200 reps/measurement, min-of-20-warmup then min-of-reps:

- Per-leaf speedups (t_seq/t_coop) are healthy: median 2.0–3.7× at w=2/4
  across j, consistent with real parallel leaf products.
- h_w itself is noisy, including **negative medians at some (j, w=2)** cells
  — see Anomalies below.
- j64 w=4: median h_w ≈ 4.68 µs, p90 ≈ 17.7 µs.
- Local A-R1/A-R2 estimates (033_ar1/ar2 docs): median h_w 0.4–3 µs, tail up
  to ~24 µs.

So the HPC in-situ h_w sits at or slightly above the local estimate's upper
range but is not dramatically worse — the per-leaf overhead model is broadly
confirmed at HPC scale.

## Anomalies

- **Negative h_w medians at some (j, w=2) cells.** h_w is the difference of
  two O(1 µs) timings (`t_coop − t_seq/w`); at w=2 the cooperative-pull path
  is barely more expensive than the solo pull, so measurement noise (timer
  resolution, cache-state jitter, OS scheduling) can push the difference
  negative. This is timer noise on a near-zero true overhead, not a
  negative-cost mechanism — consistent with A-R1's median estimate as low as
  0.4 µs.
- **Round 0 = warmup, excluded from all analysis above.** The harness writes
  one warmup row per arm (`phase=warmup`, includes a `bc_rel_l2` certification
  against the w=1 anchor) before the 5 measured trial rounds; warmup times are
  systematically longer (first-touch/compile/cache effects) and are not part
  of the paired ratio computation.
- **j1 has only w=1** (`WMAX=1` there) — no w=2/4 comparison possible at j1
  by harness design; included in the table for completeness (serial anchor).

## Interpretation

Per-leaf cooperative products *do* show real, healthy speedups (median
2–3.7×) when measured in isolation via the in-situ probe — this reconfirms
A-R1's kernel-level finding. But that per-leaf gain is not visible at the
whole-solve level: paired end-to-end timing shows flat-to-negative gain at
every j, worsening (not improving) as j grows to 64. The loss must therefore
live outside the leaf-product kernel itself — in synchronization/scheduling
overhead on the team's critical path (team start/stop, dispatch, barrier
wait, reduction bookkeeping, contention among concurrently-scheduled workers)
that the in-situ probe's isolated measurement does not capture, consistent
with A-T2's caution that elapsed coordination cost and total worker cost are
distinct quantities. This is the same "no gain, work-bound at low j" pattern
A-R2 found locally at j=4, now shown to persist and worsen through j=64 on
real HPC hardware — i.e., not a local-machine artifact.
